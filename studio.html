One sentence, one clip. The timing problem disappears.

The speaking trainer needed a speech recogniser to find out when each line began, because the audio was produced as one long file. Change the unit of synthesis and that step is unnecessary: synthesise each sentence on its own and its duration is known the moment it arrives. No Whisper, no ffmpeg, no Python — so the build runs on a phone, a tablet, or any machine with a browser. Or skip the cloud entirely: the browser already ships with the same neural voices, free and instant.

The archive holds one mp3 per sentence under seg/, plus a manifest.json that lists every file with the script it speaks. Drop the folder into the repository and the dictation drill can play a clip instead of seeking inside a long recording — exact to the first millisecond, because there is nothing to seek to.

What changed, and why it is cheaper

Shift 01

The unit of synthesis

The original pipeline synthesised a whole answer, then ran a speech recogniser over the result to recover where each sentence started. Synthesising sentence by sentence inverts that: the boundary is the request.

Shift 02

No native dependencies

Forced alignment needed Python, numpy, PyAV and a Whisper checkpoint — about 1.5 GB, and desktop only. The replacement is fetch, Blob and indexedDB, all of which already exist in the tab.

Shift 03

Rebuilds get cheap

Clips are cached by voice, pace and text. Fix one sentence and only that sentence is paid for again — the rest come straight out of the local store.
