Why this works without a speech model

Point 01

The timings already exist

Each line was aligned to the waveform when the audio was produced, so a drill needs no hand-made cue points. Playback seeks to the exact millisecond the sentence begins.

Point 02

The answer key is the script

Marking is a longest-common-subsequence alignment between the words you typed and the words that were spoken. That is a string problem, not an inference problem.

Point 03

Long answers are split by sentence

Some lines run to fifty words. They are cut at sentence boundaries, and the cut times are interpolated inside the line's real start and end — accurate here because a neural voice speaks at a near-constant rate.
